Physics

Self-propulsion (active fluid)

Relaxes the local speed toward v₀ over time τ, keeping the flow direction. Speeds are in domain units/s (short side = 1). A fluid at rest has no direction, so stir it or use the quick start. The quick start switches to periodic boundaries: without walls the flow can reach |u| ≈ v₀ everywhere (and a uniform flow reaches v₀ exactly).

Long-range viscosity / alignment

Adds S∫K(|x−y|)(u(y) − u(x))dy to the local viscosity (∫K = 1, so S is a relaxation rate), or −ν_s(−Δ)su. Exact in Fourier space on periodic boundaries; with walls a zero-padded (non-wrapping) convolution.

Finite interaction speed: a point at distance r only feels u(y, t − r/vint), the velocity y had when its signal left. Information spreads no faster than vint (light cone; the short side is crossed in 1/vint s). Slider maximum = ∞ recovers the instantaneous kernel. Speeds in domain units/s, like v₀.

The equations

The fluid is incompressible with constant density ρ = 1. Its velocity u(x, t) and pressure p(x, t) obey the Navier–Stokes equations:

∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = −∇p/ρ + ν∇²u + f
∇·u = 0

The first is momentum conservation: acceleration along a fluid parcel = pressure force + viscous diffusion (viscosity ν) + external force f (your mouse). The second is the incompressibility (mass) constraint: no fluid is created or destroyed anywhere, so the flow is divergence-free.

Self-propulsion (optional, active fluid): with it switched on, the momentum equation gains the term

factive = −(1/τ) (|u| − v₀) u/|u|

which pushes the local speed |u| toward the target speed v₀ over the relaxation time τ while keeping the local direction u/|u|: slow fluid is accelerated, fast fluid is braked (as in Toner–Tu / Vicsek models of swimming bacteria or flocks). It is applied exactly, |u|new = v₀ + (|u| − v₀)·e−Δt/τ, so it is stable for any Δt/τ. The projection afterwards keeps ∇·u = 0, so |u| ends up close to, but not exactly, v₀ everywhere: the result is "active turbulence".

Long-range viscosity / alignment (optional): the local viscous term ν∇²u only couples neighbouring points. A nonlocal kernel is added on top:

fK(x) = S ∫ K(|x − y|) (u(y) − u(x)) dy, ∫K = 1

so each point relaxes toward a K-weighted average of the velocity around it at rate S (a velocity-alignment / flocking interaction). Kernels: Gaussian K ∝ e−r²/2R² (for small R this is ordinary viscosity with ν = S R²/2); Cucker–Smale K ∝ (1 + r²/R²)−β (Cucker & Smale 2007), with an optional Motsch–Tadmor normalisation (divide by the local kernel mass, which only matters near walls); power law K ∝ (r² + R²)−α/2. The fractional Laplacian −νs(−Δ)su (0 < s ≤ 1; s = 1 is ordinary viscosity) damps short waves less and long waves relatively more. All of them conserve momentum on periodic boundaries and only ever remove kinetic energy. Combined with self-propulsion they give a Toner–Tu-like active "flock": the fluid moves at ≈ v₀ and the kernel aligns its direction over distance R.

Finite interaction speed (optional, retarded alignment): signals travel at speed vint, so the velocity received from distance r is r/vint old:

fK(x, t) = S ∫ K(r) (u(y, t − r/vint) − u(x, t)) dy, r = |x − y|, tdel = r/vint

as for the retarded potentials of electrodynamics, and as in delayed Cucker–Smale models. The kernel is split into M equal-mass radial shells (plus a tail shell holding the last 0.5 % of its mass); shell m uses the delay of its outer radius, so no signal outruns vint. Past velocity fields are kept in a ring buffer (on the torus as their Fourier spectra) and linearly interpolated in time, so the received field is Σm Km * u(t − τm) + K(0)u(x, t), updated semi-implicitly, u ← (u + SΔt·w)/(1 + SΔt·c), which is a convex combination of present and past values and therefore stable for any Δt and vint. vint → ∞ gives back the instantaneous kernel. Delays slow down (but do not prevent) flocking. The total momentum lags behind, and only momentum plus the "in-flight" part S Σ μm ∫t−τmt u ds is conserved. Pressure still acts instantly (the fluid is incompressible), so the light cone applies to the alignment interaction, not to the whole flow.

Boundaries: no-slip walls (fluid sticks to the walls, u = 0), free-slip walls (no flow through the walls, but it slides along them), periodic (a torus: whatever leaves on one side re-enters on the opposite side, in x and y, so there are no walls at all) and periodic in x, walls in y (an endless channel with no-slip walls at the top and bottom). With periodic boundaries the total momentum has nowhere to go and a uniform flow simply keeps going. The pressure Poisson equation wraps around as well, and is then solved exactly with FFTs, so ∇·u = 0 to round-off.

The coloured dye c is a passive scalar carried by the flow:

∂c/∂t + (u·∇)c = κ∇²c

Method (Stam's "Stable Fluids"): each step adds forces, diffuses velocity implicitly, advects it semi-Lagrangianly (trace back along the flow, interpolate), applies the optional self-propulsion term, and projects it onto divergence-free fields by solving the Poisson equation ∇²p = ∇·u/Δt and subtracting Δt∇p. Pressure therefore acts as the Lagrange multiplier that enforces ∇·u = 0. Everything is unconditionally stable, so large Δt never explodes (it just becomes more diffusive).
