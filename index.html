The equations

The fluid is incompressible with constant density ρ = 1. Its velocity u(x, t) and pressure p(x, t) obey the Navier–Stokes equations:

∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = −∇p/ρ + ν∇²u + f
∇·u = 0

The first is momentum conservation: acceleration along a fluid parcel = pressure force + viscous diffusion (viscosity ν) + external force f (your mouse). The second is the incompressibility (mass) constraint: no fluid is created or destroyed anywhere, so the flow is divergence-free.

The coloured dye c is a passive scalar carried by the flow:

∂c/∂t + (u·∇)c = κ∇²c

Method (Stam's "Stable Fluids"): each step adds forces, diffuses velocity implicitly, advects it semi-Lagrangianly (trace back along the flow, interpolate), and projects it onto divergence-free fields by solving the Poisson equation ∇²p = ∇·u/Δt and subtracting Δt∇p. Pressure therefore acts as the Lagrange multiplier that enforces ∇·u = 0. Everything is unconditionally stable, so large Δt never explodes (it just becomes more diffusive).
